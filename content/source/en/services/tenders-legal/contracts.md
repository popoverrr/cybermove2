# Contract drafting and review
We draft and review contracts with customers, suppliers and contractors, negotiate terms and support the deal. So risks are handled before signing, not discovered in a dispute.
TENDER · BID · CONTRACT · CORP
A template from the internet, or a contract sent by the other side, rarely reflects how the deal will actually be performed: deadlines, acceptance, payment, liability for delays, rights to the work product. Having a lawyer review a contract before signing costs less than any dispute. We read a contract not only as a legal text but as a description of a business process: what you must do, what you will receive and what happens if things do not go to plan.

## FAQ
How much does a contract review cost? |
It depends on the length and complexity of the contract and on urgency. We quote after seeing the document; the initial review is free.
How quickly can you review a contract? |
Usually within a few business days; urgent cases are discussed separately. The deadline is agreed when we receive the document.
Do you represent clients in court? |
Our job is to keep things out of court: contracts, negotiations, claims. If a dispute does go to court, we coordinate the work with specialist litigators and advocates.
Can you create contract templates for our team? |
Yes, and it saves time: for recurring deals we prepare standard contracts with clear fields and a guide on when a lawyer's review is needed.
What should a contract with a marketing agency cover? |
Scope and deliverables, metrics and how they are measured, access to accounts and data, content rights and exit terms. We cover this in detail in a separate article in Insights.
Should we review a contract sent by a large customer? |
Yes. Large customers rarely change their forms, but a review shows which risks you are accepting, where reservations are needed and what to factor into price and delivery timing.
