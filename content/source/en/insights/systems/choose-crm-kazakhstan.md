# How to choose a CRM for your sales team in Kazakhstan
How to choose a CRM for a sales team in Kazakhstan: WhatsApp, telephony, Kaspi payments, data residency, types of systems and when you don't need one yet.

Companies often choose a CRM the way they choose a phone: by reviews, by price, and by what their friends use. Three months later the sales reps are back to managing customers in notebooks and messengers, and the owner looks at reports nobody believes. It is almost never about the specific software. It is that the choice started with the software instead of the sales process. Any popular system can handle a typical pipeline — but only if you know in advance which pipeline you are moving into it.
Below: the order in which to make the decision, the criteria that matter specifically in Kazakhstan, the main classes of systems, and an honest answer to the question of when you do not need a CRM yet.
## Process first, software second
Before comparing systems, describe on one page how a sale happens in your company:
- Where enquiries come from. Website, calls, WhatsApp, Instagram, marketplaces, partners, trade shows.
- Who handles them and how fast. One person or a team; round-robin distribution or by product line.
- What stages a deal goes through. Enquiry, qualification, quote or meeting, proposal, contract, payment, delivery or service.
- Where deals are lost. At which stage customers most often drop out, and why.
- What reports management needs. Not “every possible report”, but the three to five numbers decisions are actually made on.
This description is your brief. With it, every conversation with a vendor becomes concrete: you ask not “what can your system do” but “how would I do this in it”.
## Criteria that matter in Kazakhstan
### Messengers, especially WhatsApp
In Kazakhstan a large share of customer conversations happens in WhatsApp. If those messages stay on sales reps’ phones, the CRM sees only part of the funnel. Check how a system receives messages: through the official WhatsApp Business Platform (via a certified provider) or through unofficial gateways. The official route is more stable and does not break the messenger’s rules. Check the cost too: some messages through the official platform are paid at WhatsApp’s rates, plus the provider’s fees.
### Telephony and call recording
If sales happen over the phone, a CRM without telephony is blind. Calls should automatically create or find the customer record, be recorded and be attached to the deal. Then you can review conversations, count missed calls and track callback speed. Check whether the system works with your phone operator or the IP telephony you plan to use.
### Payments: Kaspi and other methods
For many companies in Kazakhstan a significant share of payments goes through Kaspi. If payments are not linked to deals in the CRM, sales reports have to be assembled by hand. Find out whether the system has a ready integration with your payment methods, whether one can be built via API, or at least whether payments can be marked on deals according to a clear rule. This decides how honest your analytics will be.
### Personal data storage
A CRM stores customers’ personal data: names, phone numbers, conversations. Kazakhstan’s Law on Personal Data and Its Protection requires databases containing personal data to be stored in Kazakhstan. Ask the vendor where the servers physically are and what hosting options exist, and discuss this with a lawyer before signing, not after.
### Language, currency, support
An interface in Russian (and Kazakh if needed), work in tenge, documents that meet local requirements, support in your time zone. Small things that become problems if you do not ask about them up front.
## Classes of systems — without rankings
“Best CRM” rankings go out of date faster than anyone can read them and are almost always paid for by someone. It is more useful to understand the classes of systems and who they suit.
- Cloud CRMs for sales (for example Bitrix24, amoCRM/Kommo and similar). Fast to launch, ready integrations with messengers and telephony, subscription pricing. They suit most small and mid-sized sales teams. The limits appear with non-standard processes and deep links to accounting.
- CRMs built on accounting platforms (solutions on the 1C platform and similar). The strength is a single database with accounting, inventory and documents. Implementation takes longer and usually needs a contractor. They suit companies whose sales are tightly linked to stock, production and paperwork.
- International CRM platforms (HubSpot, Pipedrive, Salesforce and others). Rich features and large ecosystems. Check local integrations, payment options, data residency requirements and total cost of ownership separately.
- Custom development. Justified when the process is genuinely unique and off-the-shelf systems have been tried and did not fit. Starting with it is usually the expensive route.
Choosing between these classes is a trade-off between launch speed, flexibility and integration with accounting. There is no right answer for everyone — only the right one for your process.
## How to test a system before buying
- Run one real deal end to end. In a trial account, create an enquiry from a test WhatsApp message, make a call, issue an invoice, mark the payment. Anything that does not work becomes a question for the vendor.
- Ask them to show your report. Not a demo report — the one described in your sales process.
- Work out the total cost of ownership. Per-user subscription, paid modules, integrations, messenger fees, telephony, implementation and support. The annual total is often very different from the price on the website.
- Ask about leaving. How do you export your data if you decide to switch? A system you cannot leave is a risk.
## What to set up from day one
Settings are easy to change; data is not. Whatever was not recorded from the first day cannot be recovered later. So a few things should be built in from the start, even if you will only need the reports later.
- Lead source as a mandatory field. Without it you cannot tell which channel brings customers rather than just enquiries. For the website, the source should be filled automatically from UTM tags; for calls, from call tracking; otherwise, chosen from a short list.
- Reason for loss. When a deal closes without payment, the rep picks a reason: too expensive, not a fit, chose a competitor, could not reach. After a couple of months this becomes one of the most useful tables for marketing and sales.
- Payment amount and date. Not just “won”, but the actual amount and the date money arrived. This is what turns a CRM from a contact list into a data source for unit economics.
- Next step with a date. Every open deal has a task: what to do and when. Deals without tasks are the first to be lost.
- The same stages for every rep. If everyone understands “negotiation” differently, stage conversion means nothing. Describe each stage in one sentence: what must happen for a deal to move on.
These five elements are enough for the CRM to answer the key questions within a quarter: where customers come from, where deals are lost and how much money each channel brings.
## When you don’t need a CRM yet
Sometimes the honest answer is “not yet”. For example, if:
- there are few enquiries and one person knows every customer personally;
- there is no real sales process: every deal is unique and handled by the owner;
- nobody is ready to enter data and management will not look at reports.
In these cases a tidy spreadsheet with mandatory columns is enough: source, enquiry date, stage, amount, next step. When the spreadsheet starts getting in the way — more enquiries, more reps, customers slipping through — that is the moment for a CRM. And migration will be easy, because the data structure is already thought through.
## Why implementations fail
The reasons repeat:
- the system was configured “like everyone else’s” rather than around your process, so reps find it easier to work around it;
- some enquiries (messengers, calls to personal numbers) stayed outside the CRM;
- nobody owns the system after launch, so its settings do not change as the business changes;
- reports exist, but management does not use them, and the team notices.
A CRM works when it makes a rep’s job easier than working without it, and when management regularly makes decisions from its data.

## FAQ
How long does a CRM implementation take? |
For a small sales team with one pipeline, a basic setup takes from a few days to a couple of weeks. Integrations, data migration and getting the team used to the system take the longest. A clear description of the sales process is the best predictor: the clearer it is, the faster the launch.
Can WhatsApp be connected to a CRM? |
Yes, most systems can receive WhatsApp messages. The more reliable route is the official WhatsApp Business Platform through a certified provider; unofficial connections break the messenger's rules and risk the number being blocked.
What happens to our existing customer spreadsheets? |
They are migrated at launch: duplicates removed, fields standardised and data imported into the CRM. It is better to migrate less but carefully than to import everything and end up with a system nobody trusts.
Do we need a developer to set up a CRM? |
For a basic cloud CRM setup, usually not. A developer or integrator is needed for non-standard integrations: with accounting, a warehouse system, your own website or payment services.
